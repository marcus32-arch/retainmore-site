Your next new patient enquired at 9:05 last night.

They put their back out on the weekend, sat through Tuesday at work, and by nine they were on the couch sending the same enquiry to every clinic within ten minutes of home. Whoever answers first gets to say hello. Your answer is scheduled for whenever the front desk next gets a clear minute, and that is tomorrow.

Tue 9:05 pm Website form unread
Wed 10:41 am Front desk Inbox opened between patients. A warm reply goes back to Lauren. She booked with another clinic before opening.

No one made a mistake here. The clinic was closed, and closed is exactly when working people go looking.

The real cost

The race is over before the doors open.

  • The enquiry lands at 9:05 pm on a Tuesday. The doors locked three hours ago and the phones are on voicemail, so the form sits in an inbox nobody is looking at. The person who sent it is still on the couch with their phone, sore and motivated, sending the same message to two more clinics. The first reply is the one that gets the booking.
  • The desk finds it mid-morning, between patients. Wednesday, somewhere between an arrival and a ringing phone, someone opens the inbox and writes a proper, warm reply. It is a good reply. It is also thirteen hours late, and it lands on a person who booked somewhere else before their first coffee.
  • You never find out it happened. A lost enquiry leaves no mark. There is no gap in the diary and no complaint to field. The book looks healthy, so nothing tells you there is a leak, let alone what it costs. The only enquiries you ever meet are the ones patient enough to survive the wait.

None of it shows up anywhere you look. The diary is full, the phones were answered, the team worked hard all day. The only person who knows what Tuesday night cost is the one who enquired, and she is someone else's new patient now.

What changes

Answered at 9:05 pm. Booked by 9:30.

Run the same Tuesday again. The form arrives at 9:05 pm and the reply goes back at 9:05 pm, a personal message in your clinic's wording with your booking link inside it. She picks Thursday 2:15 pm from the couch, and by 9:30 pm she is in your book instead of someone else's. Wednesday morning the desk opens the diary to a new patient, not a stale enquiry. The arithmetic is yours to run, not ours to claim. Say a first appointment at your clinic is $120, and say a new patient who starts care comes for six visits. That puts $720 on one answered form, and you can redo the sum with your real numbers in ten seconds. Then look at last month and count the enquiries that arrived outside opening hours. Whatever that number is, it is the size of the race you are currently sitting out.

Six months on, none of this is novel any more. An enquiry that lands at 9:05 pm on a Tuesday gets its reply at 9:05 pm on that Tuesday, in the wording you approved back in week one, on the nights you are at dinner and through the fortnight you are in Queensland. Questions about times or parking get answered in the same voice, and anything beyond that waits, flagged, for your team in the morning. Once a month you read a single page: how many enquiries arrived after close, how fast each one was answered, how many ended up in your diary. The after-hours inbox stops being something you think about at all, which was the point.

  • Your wording, approved once. Every message that can go out is one you signed off before anything went live. If you would not say it at your own front desk, it never gets sent. Change a line later and it changes everywhere, and the templates stay yours if you ever leave.
  • Your rules on who gets offered what. Which appointment types and which times get offered first is set by you, and only by you. Nothing is ever sorted or prioritised by anything clinical, and nothing that goes out offers advice or comments on a condition. Anything near that line waits for a human at your clinic.
  • You keep the calendar. Bookings arrive through your own booking link, into the Nookal diary your team already runs. Nothing migrates and nobody gets a new login. Switch it off tomorrow and the clinic runs exactly as it does today.
  • Watched by us, not by you. If a reply fails at 9 pm on a Sunday, finding that is our job, not yours. We fix it at our cost and you hear about it from us first, with what happened and what changed so it stays fixed. You should never be the monitoring.

If your setup already answers at 9:05 pm, you will hear exactly that from us. Either way, you leave knowing what your after-hours enquiries actually experience.
