Booking system gaps · Power Diary and Zanda

If you run Zanda, you already have most of this.

Power Diary is now Zanda, and it is one of the better systems we look at. Waitlist suggestions, recall automations, SMS and email reminders are all built in. We are not going to pretend otherwise to sell you something.

What Zanda does natively

  • Waitlist. Instant waitlist suggestions, and clients can add themselves through the portal.
  • Recall. Automatic contact for clients who have not rebooked.
  • Reminders. Automated SMS and email, with reply confirmations.

If someone tells you those are gaps in Zanda, they have not read the feature list. That is worth knowing before you take a call from anyone in this market, including us.

So what is actually left

Two things, and neither is a missing feature.

  • Configured is not the same as running. Almost every practice we look at has some of this switched on, set up once during onboarding, and never checked since. A recall automation pointing at the wrong appointment type, or a reminder that stopped firing after a settings change, fails silently. The practice finds out from a patient, or does not find out at all.
  • Nobody is watching the outcome. Zanda will tell you a message sent. It will not tell you whether your no-show rate moved, whether the waitlist is actually clearing, or which of your automations is doing nothing. That is a reporting job that sits above the software.

The one genuine gap

Review and feedback requests are not part of what Zanda advertises. If asking patients for a review is currently a thing someone remembers to do by hand, or does not do, that is a real hole, and it is the one piece of new machinery worth building here.

It also has to be built carefully. Anything sent in your name is bound by AHPRA's advertising rules, which is why our templates never solicit testimonials about clinical outcomes and never fire on a blanket timer.

Being straight with you

If your Zanda automations are set up properly and someone checks them, you probably do not need us for retention, and we will say that on the call rather than after an invoice. Where we are worth the money is when nobody owns this: when it was configured during onboarding, the person who set it up has left, and no one can tell you whether it is still working.

That is an honest, smaller pitch than the one we would make to a practice on a weaker system. It is also the true one.

Checking your own numbers

Open Zanda and look at your automations. For each one, answer two questions: when did it last actually send, and did anyone check what happened next. If you cannot answer the second for any of them, that is the gap, and it is not a software problem.
