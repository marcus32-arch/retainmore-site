You sent it two weeks ago. Tonight you ask again.

In an NDIS practice the work is not finished when the session ends. It is finished when the agreement comes back signed and the email comes back answered, and neither happens without a nudge. So the nudging waits for you, after hours, at the kitchen table.

Fri 7 Aug, 5:15 pm Nobody Unsigned
Thu 13 Aug, 8:47 pm You You find it at the kitchen table, working down the list, and write the chase yourself

Ten days for one agreement, and it moved only because your Thursday night got longer. Everything else out for signature is on the same clock.

The real cost

The chasing is its own job, and nobody rostered it.

  • Sent is not signed. The agreement lands in a parent's inbox between school notes and shift rosters, and it sits. Nobody is refusing. They are busy, and busy people sign when someone nudges. The nudge is you, remembering it exists and writing it kindly enough to keep the relationship warm.
  • The people you chase are drowning too. The support coordinator with sixty participants, the parent between shifts. Nobody is ignoring you on purpose, which is exactly why one ask is never enough, and why the second and third asks live nowhere but your memory. A follow-up that lives in your head always loses to the client in front of you, so it waits for Thursday.
  • Ten minutes a nudge adds up. Each chase means re-reading the thread and finding words that press without pushing. Call it ten minutes. Say an agreement takes three nudges to come home and five are out at once. That is two and a half hours, before you touch the unreturned emails. Those numbers are made up on purpose. Count last month's list and put in your own.

None of it is clinical work and none of it shows up anywhere. The sessions get delivered and the notes get written, and the chasing happens in the margins, off the clock. From the outside the practice looks fine. The kitchen table knows different.

What changes

The chasing runs without you in it.

Now run the same agreement again. It goes out on the Monday. Thursday arrives with no signature, and a follow-up goes out on its own, worded the way you would word it, because you wrote it once and approved it up front. If it is still quiet next week, the next nudge goes, a touch warmer, still yours. Unreturned emails get the same patient treatment on the same kind of schedule. You see what went out and what came back, and the first time you touch any of it is when something genuinely needs a human decision.

Your evening never heard about it.

Run that forward six months. Every agreement leaves with its follow-ups already queued behind it, spaced days apart, in wording you signed off one quiet afternoon back at the start. Second and third asks go out without anyone keeping a list, and each reply pulls the next nudge out of the queue, so nobody gets chased after they have answered. The ones that come back on the first nudge, you never hear about. The stubborn ones surface to you with the whole history attached, so the one message you write is the only one that needed you. Thursday night stops being the chasing shift and goes back to being Thursday night, and the practice does not notice anything, because nothing stopped moving.

  • Your wording, approved once, up front. Every message that can ever go out is one you read and signed off before anything switched on. If you would not send it, it does not exist. The templates stay yours if you ever leave, and it is month to month, no lock-in.
  • Your rules on ordering, never anything clinical. You decide how soon the first nudge goes and when it stops and comes to you instead. Ordering runs on how long something has been waiting, never on anything about a person's condition or care. That line does not move.
  • You keep the calendar. Nothing here touches an appointment. The chasing gets paperwork signed and emails answered. Who is seen, and when, stays exactly where it is now, with you and your team.
  • Breaks are ours to find and fix. If a follow-up stops going out or goes out wrong, finding that is our job, not yours, and the fix happens at our cost. You hear it from us before you notice it yourself. You should never be the monitoring.
