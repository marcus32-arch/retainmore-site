Talk to Marcus
For solo practices running Halaxy

The last client leaves at five. The admin starts.

In a practice with no front desk, every unsigned form, every plan review and every enquiry that arrives after hours lands on the same person who ran the sessions all day. That work is real and it is unpaid, and most of it is the kind a computer should have been doing.

What it actually consists of

Four places the hours go

  • Paperwork that never came back Intake forms, consent, service agreements. They go out, some return, and the ones that do not are invisible until the client is in the room. The chasing is manual, it is awkward, and it happens at night.
  • The review between session six and session seven Under Better Access a referral covers an initial course of up to six services. The subsequent course, up to the cap of ten in a calendar year, needs the client to go back to their GP first. Nobody is watching that line. The client finishes six, the review does not get booked, and they do not come back for the other four.
  • Plan and referral review dates Sitting in Halaxy, visible if you go looking, surfaced to nobody. The session count resets on 1 January whether or not anyone noticed, and a client sitting on seven of ten in November either uses three or loses them.
  • Enquiries that arrive after you close Someone decides at nine on a Sunday night that this is the year they do something about it. By Tuesday morning, when the reply goes out, that feeling has passed.
Before we talk

Three numbers worth having in your head

None of these need a report built. They are all a few minutes in Halaxy, and having them turns a conversation about admin in general into a conversation about yours.

  • How many active clients have used six or more sessions this calendar year? That is the group sitting at the review wall right now.
  • How many intake or consent documents are still outstanding? Not how many you sent. How many came back.
  • How long does a new enquiry usually wait for its first reply? Measure from when it arrived, not from when you saw it.

Whatever those three numbers turn out to be, they are yours and they stay yours. The point of the session is to size them, not to be surprised by them.

Worth saying plainly

Some of this Halaxy already does

Appointment reminders, recall and basic no-show follow-up are in there. If they are switched on and working, there is nothing to sell you and you should not pay twice for them. A good part of any first conversation is establishing which of these is genuinely running and which has been sitting half configured since setup.

What is not covered by default is the watching. Nothing in a practice management system is counting who is approaching a review, whose paperwork never came back, or how long an after-hours enquiry sat before someone answered it.

The constraint this is built inside

Why none of it fires on a timer

Anything sent in your practice name is advertising of a regulated health service under the National Law, and AHPRA is explicit that it makes no difference whether you wrote it or a third party did. Section 133 prohibits encouraging the indiscriminate or unnecessary use of health services, which is what a blanket time-based recall to lapsed clients is.

So nothing here runs on "it has been eight weeks". It runs on a signal a clinician set: a review date on a plan, a referral expiry, a flag you put on the file. Clients are never ranked by condition, only by whether a date has passed or a document is missing. Clinical judgement stays where it legally has to stay, with you.

Patient data never reaches a model provider, because the part that runs every day is deterministic. That is a property of how it is built rather than a policy that can drift.
