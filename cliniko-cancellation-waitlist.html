Booking system gaps · Cliniko

What happens in Cliniko when someone cancels?

Short version: the appointment comes off the calendar, and that is the end of it. Nothing goes out to anyone else. The hour stays empty unless a person notices and does something about it.

What Cliniko does natively

Cliniko handles the cancellation itself properly. The appointment is removed, the slot returns to your availability, and if you have online booking switched on the time becomes bookable again to anyone who happens to visit your booking page.

That last part matters, because it is the bit people mistake for a waitlist. An open slot on a booking page is passive: it works only if the right patient happens to look at the right moment. A cancellation at eight in the evening for a nine o'clock the next morning almost never finds that person.

What it does not do

Cliniko does not maintain a waitlist of patients who want an earlier appointment, and it does not contact anybody when a slot opens. There is no native "offer this time to the next person" step. Whether the hour gets refilled depends on somebody at the front desk noticing the gap, deciding who to ring, and having time to do it.

On a Tuesday morning that is achievable. At 4pm on a Friday, or overnight, or during a school-holiday rush, it usually is not, and the slot simply expires.

Why the timing is the whole problem

Cancellations cluster at exactly the times nobody is free to act on them: late afternoon, after hours, and first thing in the morning. The appointment itself may be a day or two away, which means it was eminently fillable, but the window to fill it passes while the clinic is closed.

Clinic capacity is perishable in a way stock is not. A Tuesday 2pm that goes unsold is not sitting in a warehouse waiting for next week. It is simply gone, and the practitioner was paid for the hour either way.

What filling it automatically actually requires

Four things, none of which exist in Cliniko on their own:

  • A list of patients who have said they would take an earlier time, kept current rather than living in someone's head or a sticky note.
  • Something watching the calendar for cancellations as they happen, at any hour.
  • A message that goes out fast enough to matter, offering the specific slot, with a way to claim it that does not require a phone call back.
  • A rule for what happens when two people say yes, and what happens when nobody does.

The messages themselves are ordinary. The work is in the watching, the ordering, and the handling of the awkward cases, which is why it tends not to get built in-house even by clinics that are perfectly capable of it.

Checking your own numbers

Cliniko will tell you how many appointments were cancelled last month. Compare that with how many of those slots were filled by someone else. The difference, multiplied by your standard fee, is what the gap costs you, and it is your figure rather than ours.

If a patient stops attending altogether after a cancellation that never got rebooked, the loss is larger again, because it is the rest of their course of care rather than one appointment.
