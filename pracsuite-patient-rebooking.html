Nobody rings to tell you they're not coming back.

A patient finishes a course of care, thanks the desk on the way out and means it when they say they'll be back if they need you. Six weeks later there's nothing in the book under their name, and nobody has noticed, because a person leaving quietly makes no sound. This page is about that patient, and about the Monday list that was supposed to catch them.

Finished care, 12 days ago Nothing in the book Rebooked · nobody at the desk
Appointments Monday, 8 am
8:03 am Front desk Phones on. Two reschedules and a new patient enquiry before the kettle has boiled.
8:15 am Last week's review No one assigned
5:40 pm The same list Still eleven names. Moved to next Monday's pile.

Nobody chose to drop them. The list just never beat the phones, and next Monday it won't either.

The real cost

The list that never beats the phones.

  • They leave happy, which is exactly why they vanish. The last session ends warmly. Call us if it plays up, and they mean to. But from that moment the follow-through belongs entirely to a busy person with no reminder, and staying away feels to them like good news. Nobody's job is to notice that they never rang.
  • Monday finds them. The phones take them back. Someone runs last week's numbers and there it is, a stack of names with finished care and no future booking. Then the door opens and the phones start. Ringing eleven people who may not pick up loses to the one person standing at the desk, every day it's tried. So the list waits for a quiet afternoon that never arrives.
  • The cost turns up a quarter after the cause. One person drifting changes nothing about this week, so on the day it happens it never feels worth fighting for. The empty slots arrive months later, spread thinly across the calendar, and by then no one can say which goodbye they trace back to. What could have been a two-line message in August becomes a marketing question in November.

None of this looks like neglect. The room was full, the goodbyes were warm, someone even ran the report. It just needed a spare hour the week never had, and it needed it every single week.

What changes

The nudge goes out whether Monday was flat out or not.

Picture the same week with one difference. A patient finishes care, and after a fair stretch of days with nothing in the book, a short message goes out under your clinic's name, in wording you approved, with your booking link in it. The ones who are travelling well say so, or say nothing, and are left in peace. The ones who meant to call get the excuse they were waiting for, and a booking lands in your calendar days later without anyone at the desk touching it. Run the arithmetic on your own numbers: say a returning patient books four visits, and say your fee is $90. That is $360 riding on one short message. Count the names on your own last-month list and multiply. They're your figures, not ours, and they'll tell you more than any claim we could make.

Booked · Wed 10:15 am
Six days out. The desk heard about it from the calendar.

Run it forward six months. The Monday review still happens, but it reads like a record instead of a to-do list. A patient who finished care in March got her message within the fortnight, replied on the Thursday, and was back in the book the following Wednesday, and the desk found out from the calendar. The ones who were done stayed done, politely, and were not messaged again. Nobody carried names in their head over a weekend, and nobody stayed back at five o'clock to ring a list. The quiet weeks and the flat-out weeks got exactly the same follow-through, which is the part a busy practice can never promise itself.

  • Your wording, approved once. Every message that can ever go out is one you read and signed off before anything went live. If you wouldn't say it at the desk, it doesn't get sent. Change your mind on a phrase and it changes everywhere, that day.
  • Your rules on who hears from you, and nothing clinical in them. Who gets a nudge and when is set by rules you approve, built on booking history, things like how long since the last visit. Never on a condition or anything in a treatment note. That line does not move.
  • The calendar stays yours. Patients book through your own booking link, into availability you already publish. Nothing gets moved or double-booked on your behalf, and the desk can see everything that went out and everything that came back.
  • If it breaks, that's ours. If messages stop going out, finding that is our job, not yours. We fix it at our cost, and you hear about it from us before you'd have noticed. You should never be the monitoring.
