For practices running Halaxy

Every practice has a list nobody is watching.

The clients approaching the end of their current course of sessions. The agreements that went out and never came back. The enquiry that arrived on Sunday night. None of it is written down anywhere, all of it has consequences, and in most practices it belongs to nobody in particular.

What you get

The list gets watched, and acted on

  • Nobody reaches the end of a course of sessions unnoticed Every client approaching the limit of their current referral appears on a list before they get there, early enough for reception to do something about it. Care continues instead of stopping at a paperwork step nobody saw coming.
  • Paperwork is back before the appointment, not chased during it Intake forms, consent and service agreements follow themselves up until they return. What is still outstanding is visible on a screen rather than discovered when the client is already in the room or when a claim will not go through.
  • An enquiry at nine on a Sunday is answered by nine fifteen Not Monday, not Tuesday. In your wording, with the next real availability in it.
  • Cancelled slots go to the people who asked to be called The moment the slot opens, not the next morning when reception gets to it.
  • One view across every caseload What came in, what got answered, what booked, who is approaching a review. Across the whole practice, not practitioner by practitioner.

Whatever a session is worth in your practice, multiply it by the number of clients sitting at that review line right now across every caseload. That figure is yours and it is one report away. We will pull it together on the call rather than guess at it here.

Why it goes unwatched today

It falls between the desk and the chair

This is not a staffing problem, and adding people does not fix it. Reception is dealing with whoever is at the counter, on the phone or arriving early. That is the right call every time, because the person in front of you is real and a review date three weeks out is not.

The practitioner is in session. They see their own caseload, not the pattern across the practice, and not the client who quietly stopped booking in March.

So the dates pass. Not through anyone's failure, but because watching them was never assigned to a person, and in most practices it has never been assigned to a system either.

What is actually on the list

Four things nothing is counting

  • Clients at the end of a course of sessions Under the Better Access initiative a referral covers an initial course of up to six services, and the remainder of the ten available in a calendar year needs the client to return to their referring practitioner first. They finish the first course, that visit never gets booked, and the rest of the entitlement is never used.
  • Plans that have aged out Under a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan, which replaced GP Management Plans and Team Care Arrangements on 1 July 2025, a client stays eligible for allied health services only if their plan was prepared or reviewed within the previous 18 months. Plans written before that date run until 30 June 2027, so both regimes are live at once and the dates sit in different places.
  • Paperwork that never came back You know what went out. Nothing tells you what returned, so it surfaces at the worst possible moment.
  • Enquiries that arrive after you close Someone decides at nine on a Sunday that this is the year they do something about it. By the time a reply goes out on Tuesday the feeling has passed, and they have usually contacted someone else as well.
Before we talk

Three numbers worth pulling

None of these need a report built. They are a few minutes in Halaxy, and having them turns a general conversation about admin into a specific one about your practice.

  • How many active clients have used six or more sessions this calendar year? That is the group sitting at the review line right now, across every caseload.
  • How many intake or consent documents are still outstanding? Not how many went out. How many came back.
  • How long does a new enquiry wait for its first reply? Measured from when it arrived, not from when someone saw it.
The usual objection

Available and working are different things

Most practice management systems list reminders and recall among their features, and most practices have exactly that sitting half configured since the day they were onboarded. A feature that exists in a settings page is not the same as one that is switched on, pointed at the right people, worded the way you would word it, and checked by someone when it stops firing.

Part of the first conversation is establishing which of it is genuinely running in your setup. Where something is working, we say so and build around it rather than over it.

The watching is the part no practice management system does at all. Nothing is counting who is approaching a review, whose paperwork never came back, or how long an enquiry sat before a human reached it. That gap is the same whether you have two practitioners or twenty, except that it scales with you.

The constraint this is built inside

Why none of it fires on a timer

Anything sent in a practice's name is advertising of a regulated health service under the National Law, and AHPRA is explicit that it makes no difference whether the practice wrote it or a third party did. Section 133 prohibits encouraging the indiscriminate or unnecessary use of health services, which is what a blanket time-based recall to lapsed clients is.

So nothing here runs on "it has been eight weeks". It runs on a signal a clinician set: a review date on a plan, a referral that is running out, a document genuinely outstanding. Clients are never ranked by condition, only by whether a date has passed or something is missing. Clinical judgement stays where the law requires it.

The part that runs every day is deterministic, so patient data never reaches a model provider. That is a property of the architecture rather than a policy, which means it cannot quietly drift.
