The slot is empty. Eleven people wanted it.

A late cancellation on a Friday afternoon is the most expensive kind. Everyone who could fill it is about to go home, and the people who asked to be called are never told. That's the hour your week quietly loses, and getting it back shouldn't depend on anyone being free at 4:38 pm.

Appointments Thursday
1:00 pm Alira N. Review
2:00 pm Jordan P. Cancelled Fri 4:38 pm
3:00 pm Meera S. Standard

Eleven people on the waiting list. None of them were told.

The real cost

One empty hour. Three people paying for it.

  • The clinic pays first. The room is lit, the practitioner is rostered, and the hour bills nothing. Six late-notice cancellations a month at $160 a session is close to $1,000 of empty room time, every month, in a practice that's turning people away. Your numbers will be different, and they're one report away.
  • The person on the list pays next. They asked to be called. They'd have said yes in a heartbeat, rearranged their afternoon, and thanked you for it. Instead they wait another fortnight for care they were ready to start on Thursday.
  • Then it lands on you. Because filling the slot means someone ringing one person, waiting to hear back, ringing the next. On a busy front desk that job loses to the patient standing at the counter every single time, and by Monday the hour is history.
What changes

Friday, handled by Friday.

Picture the same afternoon with nobody at the desk doing anything. The cancellation comes off the calendar, and within a couple of minutes the right people from your list have a message in their hand, in your wording. First yes takes the slot. You find out it was filled, not that it was empty.

2:00 pmJordan P. Thursday Cancelled 4:38 pm
Hi Nadia, a 2:00 pm Thursday has just opened up with Alex. You are on our waiting list for a Thursday afternoon. Reply YES and it is yours.
Sent to waiting list · 4:40 pm
Yes please!
Slot filled · Nadia K. 5:02 pm

Run that forward a few months and the shape of the practice changes. The list finally moves, so the people on it start sooner and stop ringing to ask where they're up to. The book stays full without anyone pushing it. And the person who used to do the chasing, which in most clinics is you, gets that time back for the work only you can do.

  • Your wording, approved once. You sign off the messages up front, not one by one. What goes out is what you approved, and we keep the approval on file.
  • Your rules on who gets asked. Ordered by fit to the slot and how long someone has waited. Never by anything clinical, ever.
  • You still hold the calendar. The system does the chasing and hands you the yes. Nothing books itself without you.
  • When something breaks, that's ours. We find it and fix it at our cost, and you hear it from us before you'd have noticed.
