Booking system gaps · Halaxy

Halaxy has a waiting list. That is not the same as it being used.

This page is not going to tell you Halaxy is missing a feature it has. The waiting list is real, it is good, and it does what it says. The gap is somewhere else, and it is the kind that does not show up in a feature comparison.

What Halaxy actually does

You can add a patient to a waiting list, set their preferences for practitioner, location, time of day and days of the week, and flag the urgent ones so they sit at the top. When a slot opens you move someone into it.

That is a proper waiting list. Plenty of systems do it worse.

Where it stops

Moving a patient off the list and into the cancelled slot is a thing a person does. Someone opens the list, decides who fits, contacts them, waits to hear back, and books them in.

  • It needs someone at the screen. The list does not reach out on its own. Nothing happens until a human opens it and starts making contact.
  • The timing is the whole problem. A Tuesday morning cancellation has a day to be filled. A Friday 4pm cancellation has about twenty minutes of realistic attention before everyone has gone home, and that is the one that stays empty.
  • Preferences make it slower, not faster. Matching who actually fits a Thursday 2pm with this practitioner at this site is a judgement call each time, which is exactly the sort of small decision that gets deferred when the desk is busy.
  • First reply wins. Contacting one person and waiting means the slot sits idle while you wait. Contacting several at once needs someone to manage the race.

Why this matters more in your disciplines

If you are running a waiting list at all, you are turning people away. That changes the arithmetic. An unfilled cancellation is not a quiet hour, it is a session someone on the list was waiting for and did not get, while you carried the cost of the room anyway.

What we do about it

This is our own conduct, so we will be specific.

  • The slot triggers the outreach, not a person noticing. When an appointment comes off the calendar, contact goes out against the criteria you set, within minutes, at 4pm on a Friday as readily as Tuesday morning.
  • You approve the wording once, up front. Not message by message. What goes out is what you signed off, and we keep the approval on file.
  • Sensible about who gets asked. Ordered by your rules, not ours, and never by anything clinical. We rank by fit to the slot and how long someone has waited, never by condition.
  • You still book it. The system does the chasing and hands you the person who said yes. Nothing writes into your calendar without you.
  • When it breaks, that is ours. If Halaxy changes something and the connection stops, we find it and fix it at our cost, and you hear it from us first.

Checking your own numbers

Open Halaxy and count last month's cancellations, then count how many were filled by someone from the waiting list. The gap between those two is the only figure that matters here, and it is yours, not a number we made up. Multiply it by your standard fee.

We are not going to tell you what share of it comes back. That depends on your list, your hours and how far ahead you book. Working it out honestly is what the fifteen minutes is for.
