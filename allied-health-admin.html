For psychology, OT, speech pathology and exercise physiology

Your waitlist is full. Your evenings are not your own.

Most clinics we talk to in NDIS-funded work do not have a patient problem. They have a paperwork problem, and it is being paid for in clinical hours.

The maths is different here

A clinic with gaps in the diary loses money to empty chairs. A clinic with a waitlist does not have that problem, so the usual pitch about filling slots is beside the point.

What it has instead is an hour that went on evidence-gathering rather than a session, with somebody waiting for that appointment. That hour is not idle time, it is a session that could have happened and did not. Which is why admin here costs more than it does in a practice with a half-empty Tuesday.

What actually eats the time

  • Keeping up with what changed. Line items get restructured, item names get rewritten, guidance arrives in pieces. You are treating all day, and staying current is a second job nobody scheduled.
  • Audit readiness. Either an outside consultant bills for it, or you spend your own weekends assembling documents that already exist somewhere in your systems.
  • Expiry and currency. Staff training, insurance, first aid, checks, policy review dates, plan and agreement end dates. All knowable in advance, all discovered late.
  • Evidence that lives in six places. The information is not missing. It is scattered, and assembling it is the work.

What we take off your desk

We build and run the layer that does the assembling, connected to the system you already use. Nothing to migrate, no new login for your team.

  • Expiry and currency tracked in one place, flagged before it lapses rather than after.
  • Evidence gathered on a schedule, so it is assembled continuously instead of in a panic.
  • Document versions and review dates kept current, with an audit trail that writes itself.
  • Plan and service-agreement dates watched, with the prompt going out in time to act.

What we do not do, and will not pretend to

We are not your auditor and we are not a compliance consultancy. We do not write your policies, interpret a standard on your behalf, or tell you that you will pass an audit. That is judgement work, it belongs with someone qualified to carry it, and anyone promising otherwise to a practice they have never seen is selling you something.

What we do is make sure that when that person turns up, or an auditor does, the evidence is already there and current. Most of the recurring cost is the gathering, not the judgement.

Working out whether it is worth it

Two numbers you already have. How many hours a week go on compliance and admin that is not clinical. And what an hour of your clinical time bills at.

Multiply them. That is the ceiling on what this is worth to you, and it is your figure, not ours. We will not tell you what share of it is recoverable, because that depends on your systems and your setup, and working it out properly is what the fifteen minutes is for.
